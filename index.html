How to play Spettro

Start

Spettro is a spectral synth: an oscillator feeds a bank of up to eight resonant band filters, each with its own envelope and its own delay line. The sound engine is the instrument's own firmware running in the browser.

  • Click or tap the panel once to switch the audio on. Headphones or good speakers help: much of the sound is in the low and the stereo bands.
  • schermo intero leaves only the instrument on the screen; Esc (or the system control on a tablet) brings the bar back.
  • Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all play it; MIDI needs Chrome, Edge or Firefox on a computer.
  • Crackles? Hold system for 2 s and open setup: audio load shows how busy the engine is, and a tap on latency low switches to a larger, safer audio buffer (the page reloads; tap again to go back).

Controls

  • Knobs: drag up or down. Faders: drag along the slot, or touch the slot to jump there.
  • shift while dragging for fine steps, the mouse wheel for single steps, double click for the initial value.
  • Buttons work as on the instrument: a short tap and a long press often do different things (listed below).
  • Rings and fader bars light yellow up to the value.

Playing the pads

  • The 24 pads (3 rows of 8) are tuned to the scale chosen on the keyboard page; five voices are shared by pads and MIDI.
  • Pressure: a mouse, a finger or the PC keyboard play as a key pressed fully. A pen or an Apple Pencil plays with its real pressure; the comb trimmer sets how sensitive it is. Touch screens cannot measure finger pressure.
  • Slide: keep the finger down and move it towards a neighbouring pad (same row, or the two touching it in the next row): the note bends towards that pad's pitch; three quarters of the way the note moves onto it without a new attack, and you can keep sliding. While you slide, the note's own pad lights violet, the two pads under the finger pulse turquoise and the pads you crossed stay dimly lit. Not with the arpeggiator on.
  • −2 −1 +1 +2 octave: a tap changes octave (tap the lit one again to go back to 0); held for a moment it changes octave only while you hold it.
  • Diagonal strip: transpose by scale degrees, ±12. Tuning-fork trimmer: fine tune.

Hold and arpeggio

  • ❄ Hold — tap: latch, the notes keep playing after you lift (LED red); tap again to release them. Hold it while playing: the notes under your fingers are captured as a drone until you let go of ❄ (LED violet). The 12 LEDs by the transpose fader show it: up in yellow from the bottom, down in red from the top. During the drone they turn violet and the transpose fader becomes a stutter that repeats the end of the sound going into the delay and reverb, so their echoes and tails build on the repeats, while whatever else you play sounds over it. The fader has 12 steps from the bottom (one violet LED each): the plain drone, then 1/2, 1/4., 1/4, 1/8., 1/4T, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32, 1/64 of the beat; the screen shows the step. Letting go of ❄ stops it and gives the fader back to transpose; the step is kept, so the next drone stutters again at once.
  • arp — tap: arpeggiator on or off. Long press: the arpeggio page; its first item, mode, chooses arp or seq (the sequencer, below). Then tempo division, pattern, gate length, groove and its amount. With the arpeggio on, Hold pauses and restarts it.
  • clock — tap it in time to set the tempo; long press: the tempo page. The LED blinks on the beat.

Sequencer

  • Long press arp and set mode to seq. The menu also sets the length (1–24 steps, 16 to start), the step (1/4 to 1/32, triplets too), the direction (forward, backward, pendulum, random), swing, whether live recording adds to the steps or replaces them, and clear (click twice).
  • The 24 pads become the steps (arp LED green, lit only in this view): blue the steps with notes (dim when off), yellow the one playing, dark past the length. Tap a step: on or off (an empty one gets the last note you played). Hold a step: its page opens (notes, pressure, gate, tie, on/off) and you hear it once. Hold ❄ and tap a step: the length ends there.
  • ❄ in the step view is ▷ □: tap to play or stop (LED green while it plays), tap twice quickly to start again from the beginning. With MIDI clock it starts on the beat and follows Start, Stop and Continue.
  • Octaves: the sequence and the keyboard have their own. In the step view the octave buttons move the sequence (their LED green); on the keyboard, and while recording, they move only what you play. Each step keeps the octave it was recorded at. Transpose moves both.
  • Tap arp: the pads go back to playing notes over the sequence (arp LED off) and ❄ is Hold and drone again. The sequence steals voices from itself, never from what you play. Tap arp again for the steps.
  • Hold ❄ there while the sequence plays: its notes become the drone too (caught between two notes, the last step sounds again), so the transpose fader stutters the sequence. It keeps time silently meanwhile: let go of ❄ and it plays on, in step.
  • Record: hold arp and press ‹ (keys 5 + 6; the ‹ LED red, blinking during the count-in). Stopped, it records step by step from step 1: each pad or chord writes a step, ❄ writes a rest, a pad held + ❄ ties the note into the next step. Playing, it records live after one bar of count-in (the pads pulse on the beat), quantized to the nearest step; and every control you move records a motion cycle as long as the pattern, in time with it, replacing that control's old cycle. Tap ‹ or arp to stop recording. While recording, the top of the screen shows the steps (lighter with notes, red where it is, each beat taller), the step number, or the beats left of the count-in.
  • The sequence, its settings and its motion cycles are saved with presets.

Oscillators

  • B fader: the main oscillator's wave. bank button: Classic, Fold, Noise or Exciter, each a different family of waves, then External (orange LED): the audio input in place of the oscillator, to play Spettro as an effect. Choose the input under audio in in the top bar (stereo is summed to mono); the B fader sets its level (half = as it comes in, top = twice as loud). Each note filters the input through its bands, then delay and reverb: hold notes (❄ or a drone) to keep the effect open.
  • A fader: the sub oscillator's wave. 1/2/4: sub divider (one, two or four octaves down); long press: sub detune.
  • blend mixes main and sub; xmod cross-modulates them; with External it ring-modulates the input with the sub, tuned to the note.

Filter bank

  • bands: how many bands, shown by the left LED chain.
  • shape spreads the bands apart, shift moves them all up or down, reso makes them narrower and more resonant.
  • mode button: how the bands are placed — fixed, harmonic, interval, inharmonic. While you play, the band chain shows each band's envelope in the mode's colour.

Envelope

  • attack and release of every note.
  • The button by them opens the band envelope page: attack and release shapes (which bands open or close first) and sustain (which bands stay open while you hold a note; the others pluck). Its LED blinks while the page is open; any other button closes it.

Delay and reverb

  • Spectral delay: Δt delay time, spread how differently each band is delayed, feedback how much of each echo comes back. At the top of feedback (the last stretch) the echoes hold: the loop stays as it is and new notes no longer enter, until you turn it down. Held bands are red on the right chain. The chain shows each band's delay against the base time: shorter, equal, longer, flashing yellow on each echo.
  • The button by Δt — tap: switch the three knobs to the reverb (LED and rings red): size from a small room to a hall (the tail lasts the same), tilt which bands feed it (left the low ones, middle all, right the high ones), diffuse how much reverb and how long; at the top it holds forever and takes no new sound. Hold it 1 s: delay normal or reverse.
  • The fx mix fader, joined to Δt: from the bottom to the middle the delay and reverb come in under the full dry sound, from the middle up the dry sound fades and only the effects are left. Double click: the middle.

LFO

  • amount and speed (white rings); the wave button cycles triangle, ramp down, ramp up, square, random (five LEDs).
  • ◐ assign — tap: the LED blinks red; now click any knob or fader to give it the LFO (or take it away) — the click does not move the control. Tap ◐ again to finish. Two quick taps without assigning clear every assignment. A modulated control shows its value plus the LFO in white (bands: the band LEDs).

Motion loop ‹

  • Tap ‹ (orange blinking) and move some controls: each one records its own cycle from its first movement. Tap ‹ again to close the cycles; they keep playing (blue rings and bars).
  • A new take re-records only the controls you move. Moving a looping control by hand removes just its cycle. Double tap ‹ clears every cycle. Loops are saved with presets.

Screen

  • Tap or click an item to select it or run it. Change a value by swiping on it (right or up raises, left or down lowers), with the < > arrows at its right edge, or with the mouse wheel. Tap the page title to go back.
  • The resting page is the spectrum analyser.
  • system — tap: the keyboard page (scale, the pitch of the last pad, root, fine tuning in cents, pad layout); tap again to go back. Hold 2 s: the System pages (MIDI channel and the four CC assignments, keyboard, setup with factory reset and preset export).

Presets

  • preset opens the preset page: choose the bank (BNK) and the preset (PRS), or tap a slot in the grid, then load or save. The pads keep playing while the page is open.
  • Bank 0 holds the 24 factory presets and cannot be overwritten; banks 1–31 are yours and stay in this browser (export them from System → setup).
  • Tapping preset twice quickly on the preset page brings back the sound you had before opening it.

MIDI

  • Choose the input and the channel in the top bar. Notes follow the pads' tuning; Hold and the arpeggiator work on MIDI notes too.
  • Four CCs can be assigned to controls (System → midi). Program change selects presets: bank = 1 + the whole part of program ÷ 24, preset = program mod 24 + 1.
  • iPad and iPhone browsers have no MIDI access.
  • The browser asks once for permission to use the audio input; the choice is remembered.

Keyboard mode

The keyboard mode button, or `, turns the PC keyboard into the pads:

  • Q…I top row, A…K middle row, Z…, bottom row.
  • 1 2 3 4 octave −2 −1 +1 +2, space Hold, 5 arp, 6 motion loop (5 held + 6: sequencer record).
  • Many PC keyboards cannot read every chord of three or more keys.
